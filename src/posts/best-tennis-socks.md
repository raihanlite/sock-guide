## FAQ

**Are tennis socks different from running socks?** Yes, in emphasis. Running is repetitive forward motion; tennis adds lateral slides and hard stops, so tennis socks pad the forefoot more heavily and prioritise staying locked in the shoe sideways. A cushioned running sock works in a pinch; a tennis sock works every match.

**Thick or thin socks for tennis?** Thick if you play on hard courts, move aggressively, or get blisters and sore forefeet — that is the Thorlos case. Thin if you prize court feel and play in heat — the Experia case. Fit matters more than either: a sliding thin sock blisters faster than a fitted thick one.

**How do I stop blisters during long matches?** Dry feet, fitted socks, broken-in shoes. Choose moisture-managing fibres (never cotton), consider a friction-reducing blend like Balega's Blister Resist, lace to lock the heel, and change into a dry pair between matches on tournament days. If damp feet are the root problem, [Why Do Socks Make Your Feet Sweat?](/posts/why-do-socks-make-your-feet-sweat/) explains the mechanism.

**Crew or no-show for tennis?** Performance is a wash; comfort decides. Crews protect the Achilles from stiff shoe collars and stay put more reliably. No-shows are cooler and suit low-collared shoes. If a no-show slips on you once, it will slip forever — switch to a quarter or crew. The full height-by-height comparison is in [Crew Socks vs Ankle Socks](/posts/crew-socks-vs-ankle-socks/).
