Sweaty feet are not a sock failure — your feet carry around a quarter of a million sweat glands between them, and sealed inside a shoe all day, that sweat has nowhere to go. The sock's job is not to stop the sweating; it is to move the moisture away from your skin and let it evaporate, instead of holding it against you for hours. The wrong sock traps sweat; the right one manages it. If you want the full explanation of why feet sweat and when it is a medical matter, start with [Why Do Socks Make Your Feet Sweat?](/posts/why-do-socks-make-your-feet-sweat/) — this guide is the buying half: six socks that actually cope, picked from 2026 guides, podiatrist advice and maker specifications.

The short version: lightweight merino for all-day wear, fast-drying synthetics for workouts and hot weather, toe socks if the sweat sits between your toes, and a silver-infused pair if odour is the main problem. Avoid high-cotton socks — cotton absorbs sweat and holds it against the skin. One note before the picks: this guide is general information about socks, not medical advice. If your feet soak through socks at rest, or the sweating interferes with daily life, see a doctor or podiatrist — the sweat guide explains the signs worth acting on.

## FAQ
<details class="faq"><summary>What socks are best for sweaty feet?</summary><div class="a">Lightweight merino blends for all-day wear, fast-drying synthetics for workouts and hot weather, and toe socks if the sweat sits between your toes. The fibre matters more than the brand: merino stores moisture inside the fibre so skin feels dry, synthetics move it out to evaporate, and cotton holds it against the skin. The full reasoning is in <a href="/posts/why-do-socks-make-your-feet-sweat/">Why Do Socks Make Your Feet Sweat?</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are merino wool socks good for sweaty feet?</summary><div class="a">Yes — merino is the most recommended fibre for sweaty feet across 2026 guides and podiatrist advice. It absorbs around 30% of its weight in moisture before feeling wet, regulates temperature in warm and cool conditions, and naturally resists the odour-causing bacteria that thrive in damp fabric. A merino blend with nylon dries faster and lasts longer than pure merino.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are bamboo socks good for sweaty feet?</summary><div class="a">They are a reasonable middle ground: bamboo viscose is soft, breathable and more absorbent than cotton, and suits sensitive skin. But it holds what it absorbs, so it dries slower than merino or a performance synthetic. For heavy sweating, merino or a synthetic wicking sock will keep feet drier; bamboo is the comfort-first choice for mild cases.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Do silver or copper socks help with foot odour?</summary><div class="a">They help with odour specifically, rather than with wetness. Silver and copper ions slow the bacteria that break sweat down into smell — which is why the Silverlight pick above exists. They cost more per pair, and they do not make feet drier on their own, so pair them with a wicking fibre (merino or synthetic) rather than relying on the metal alone.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How can I stop my feet sweating in shoes?</summary><div class="a">No sock stops feet sweating — the fixes are about managing the moisture. Change into a fresh pair midday on long or active days, rotate shoes so each pair dries fully between wears, dry between your toes before socks go on, and match sock weight to the weather: a thinner knit before anything else. The full list of what actually helps is in <a href="/posts/why-do-socks-make-your-feet-sweat/">Why Do Socks Make Your Feet Sweat?</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Should I avoid cotton socks if my feet sweat?</summary><div class="a">For sweaty feet, yes — avoid high-cotton socks for anything longer than a short, dry, low-activity wear. Cotton absorbs sweat readily and keeps it in the fabric, in contact with your skin, for hours, which is also where odour bacteria thrive. A moisture-control cotton blend (like the Dickies pick above) is better than plain cotton, but merino or a performance synthetic will keep feet drier still.</div></details>
