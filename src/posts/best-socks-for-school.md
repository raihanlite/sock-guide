School socks are bought by parents, worn by children, and judged by a dress code — which makes them a different shopping problem from your own socks. The school list usually says black, white or navy, and sometimes the length too; the child cares about whether the toe seam rubs and whether the sock stays up; and the parent cares about whether the pair survives the wash, whether there are enough of them to get through the week, and whether the drawer still matches on Friday. So this guide picks by need: the big bulk pack that fills the drawer, the plain cotton crew that actually matches a uniform, the cushioned crew for PE and recess, the unisex ten-pack for a shared drawer, the cotton crew for older kids, and the knee-high for skirts and Mary Janes. One honest note first: buy by shoe size, not by age — a children's shoe fitter who has spent a decade fitting school shoes puts it plainly, and every guide below agrees: the age range on the label is too approximate, and two children the same age in different shoe sizes need different socks. The heel pocket is what decides fit — a sock with a defined heel stays where you put it, while a straight tube bunches under the arch. For the background first — heights, fits and what each type is for — start with [Types of Socks](/posts/types-of-socks/) and [How to Choose the Right Socks](/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/). The fibre-by-fibre case for each material is in [Sock Fabrics](/posts/sock-fabrics/), the washing rules that keep a school drawer alive are in [How to Take Care of Socks](/posts/how-to-take-care-of-socks/), and the adult everyday picks are in [Best Everyday Socks](/posts/best-everyday-socks/).

The short version: Hanes Double Tough for the biggest pack at the lowest fuss, COTTON DAY for the uniform-legal plain crew, adidas for PE and sporty days, Amazon Essentials for the shared unisex drawer, Under Armour for older kids who want a proper training sock, and Jefferies for the knee-high that stays up under a skirt. One fit rule decides most of it: check the school's sock policy before you buy — colour first, then length — because a perfect sock in the wrong colour is a wasted pack.

## FAQ
<details class="faq"><summary>What socks should children wear to school?</summary><div class="a">Start with the school's policy: most schools ask for plain black, white or navy, and some specify the length too. For sneakers and lace-up school shoes, a crew sock is the practical choice — it protects the lower leg and fills the gap under the trouser leg. For Mary Jane dress shoes, an ankle sock stops the strap rubbing bare skin. If toe seams bother your child, look for a seamless or flat-toe sock; the COTTON DAY pick above has one. The full length ladder is in <a href="/posts/types-of-socks/">Types of Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How many pairs of school socks do kids need?</summary><div class="a">A week's worth is the minimum — seven pairs, one per school day, plus a couple of spares for PE days and the pair that goes missing in the wash. Ten to fourteen pairs is the comfortable number: it covers a full school week plus spares without a midweek laundry run, which is exactly why the Hanes 12-pack and the Amazon Essentials 10-pack lead this guide. Rotate them so no single pair is worn two days running, and the whole drawer lasts longer; the care rules are in <a href="/posts/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What colour socks are allowed at school?</summary><div class="a">White, black and navy are the three most common uniform sock colours, and every pick above comes in at least two of them. Some schools specify white only, others allow any solid neutral, and a few — usually Catholic and private schools — specify solid only with no logos. Check the school's policy before ordering: a perfect sock in the wrong colour is a wasted pack.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How do I find the right sock size for my child?</summary><div class="a">Use the shoe size printed on the sock tag, not the age range. Age ranges on sock labels are too approximate — an 8-year-old in a size 3 shoe and an 8-year-old in a size 6 shoe need completely different socks. Every reputable children's sock brand lists a shoe size range alongside the age range on the label; use the shoe size and ignore the age. The heel pocket is the other check: a sock with a defined heel stays where you put it, while a straight tube bunches under the arch.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What socks should kids wear for PE?</summary><div class="a">A cushioned athletic crew — the adidas pick above, or the Under Armour training crew for older kids. The cushioning sits under the foot without making the shoe tight, the synthetic knit moves sweat out faster than cotton, and the top elastic keeps the sock up through a full lesson. If PE is followed by lessons in the same shoes, a fresh pair afterwards keeps feet drier for the afternoon; the sweaty-feet thinking is in <a href="/posts/best-socks-for-sweaty-feet/">Best Socks for Sweaty Feet</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How often should I replace school socks?</summary><div class="a">Replace them when the heel or toe thins, the elastic goes dead and the cuff slides down, or the sock smells after washing — usually every few months of daily school wear, sooner than adult everyday socks because children outgrow them and wear them harder. Check the fit each term: a sock that fitted in September may be too small by January, and a too-small sock bunches under the arch and causes blisters. The replacement signs are in <a href="/posts/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a>.</div></details>
