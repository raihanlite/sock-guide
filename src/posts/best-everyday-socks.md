"Everyday" means the pair you reach for most mornings — commute, office, school run, errands, evening at home. Not a sport sock, not a boot sock: comfortable at 8 in the morning and still comfortable at 8 at night, washable several times a week, and cheap enough to wear without worry.

So this guide picks by daily need: the best all-rounder, the cheapest way to stock a drawer, the merino pair for long days, the casual pair for sneakers, the thinner pair for dress shoes, and the cotton crew. Most are sold in men's and women's versions of the same knit — women's picks in [Best Socks for Women](/best-socks-for-women/), men's in [Best Socks for Men](/best-socks-for-men/), school picks in [Best Socks for School](/best-socks-for-school/). Sweaty feet? See [Best Socks for Sweaty Feet](/best-socks-for-sweaty-feet/); the fibre case is in [Sock Fabrics](/sock-fabrics/). Background: [Types of Socks](/types-of-socks/) and [How to Choose the Right Socks](/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/).

## FAQ
<details class="faq"><summary>What socks are best for everyday wear?</summary><div class="a">A midweight crew in a cotton blend or merino, matched to the shoe and the day: cotton blend for the office and dry days, merino if your feet sweat or the day runs long. Thin no-shows belong with flats and loafers; thick hiking socks belong in boots, not slim trainers. The step-by-step choice is in <a href="/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/">How to Choose the Right Socks</a>; the fibre case is in <a href="/sock-fabrics/">Sock Fabrics</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How many pairs of everyday socks should I own?</summary><div class="a">A week's worth at minimum — seven pairs — so a clean pair is always waiting. Ten to fourteen is more comfortable: it covers laundry day, a spare pair in the bag, and rotation so each pair rests between wears. One style in one colour, bought in bulk, is the simplest drawer: pairs never go missing. <a href="/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a> covers washing and rotation.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are crew socks or ankle socks better for everyday?</summary><div class="a">Neither is better in general — it depends on the shoe and the day. Crew socks suit boots, trousers and cold weather; ankle and no-show socks suit trainers, shorts and the gym. The mistake is wearing one height for everything: a crew sock bunched into a low trainer, or an ankle sock under trousers that shows bare leg. The full comparison is in <a href="/crew-socks-vs-ankle-socks/">Crew Socks vs Ankle Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are expensive socks worth it for everyday wear?</summary><div class="a">Sometimes — but the maths depends on how long they last. A premium merino crew costs as much as a six-pack of budget socks, yet Darn Tough replaces any pair free for life. Split the drawer: a few premium pairs for the long days, a cheap multi-pack for the ordinary ones — the Dickies or Gold Toe pick above is the cheapest way to stock it.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What socks are best for standing all day?</summary><div class="a">A full-cushion crew with arch support — the Dickies pick above is the budget answer, and the merino Darn Tough crew is the premium one. Padding under the ball and heel takes the sting out of hard floors, and a sock that stays up means you stop thinking about your feet by midday. For jobs in safety boots, the work-specific picks are in <a href="/best-work-socks/">Best Work Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How often should I replace everyday socks?</summary><div class="a">Everyday socks last roughly 6 to 12 months of regular wear — replace them when they thin at the heel or toe, lose their elastic, or keep their smell after washing, whichever comes first. Washing cold and air-drying stretches that out, because heat is what kills elastic. The full care and replacement guide is in <a href="/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a>.</div></details>
