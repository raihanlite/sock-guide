"Everyday" here means the pair you reach for most mornings — the sock for the commute, the office, the school run, the errands, the evening at home. Not a sport sock, not a job-specific boot sock, not a hiking sock: the pair that has to be comfortable at 8 in the morning and still comfortable at 8 at night, survive the wash several times a week, and not cost so much that you are afraid to wear it. So this guide picks by daily need, not by brand: the best all-rounder, the cheapest way to stock a drawer, the merino pair for long days, the casual pair that looks good with sneakers, the thinner pair for dress shoes, and the classic cotton crew. Most of these socks are sold in men's and women's versions of the same knit; where the links below go to a men's listing, the women's version is the same sock in different sizes — the women's picks are in [Best Socks for Women](/posts/best-socks-for-women/), the men's in [Best Socks for Men](/posts/best-socks-for-men/). If you want the background first — heights, fits and what each type is for — start with [Types of Socks](/posts/types-of-socks/) and [How to Choose the Right Socks](/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/).

The short version: a cotton-blend half calf for the best all-round day, a cheap cushioned crew pack to stock the drawer, a merino crew for the days that run long, a casual crew that looks good with sneakers, a thin merino crew for dress shoes and the office, and a classic cotton crew that is the cheapest per pair on this page. If your feet sweat a lot, the rotation changes — the sweaty-feet picks are in [Best Socks for Sweaty Feet](/posts/best-socks-for-sweaty-feet/), and the fibre-by-fibre case for each material is in [Sock Fabrics](/posts/sock-fabrics/).

## FAQ
<details class="faq"><summary>What socks are best for everyday wear?</summary><div class="a">A midweight crew in a cotton blend or merino, matched to the shoe and the day: a cotton blend for the office and dry days, merino if your feet sweat or the day runs long. Thin, smooth no-shows belong with flats and loafers; thick hiking socks belong in boots, not in slim trainers. The step-by-step choice is in <a href="/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/">How to Choose the Right Socks</a>, and the fibre-by-fibre case is in <a href="/posts/sock-fabrics/">Sock Fabrics</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How many pairs of everyday socks should I own?</summary><div class="a">A week's worth at minimum — seven pairs — so a clean pair is always waiting. Ten to fourteen pairs is more comfortable: it covers laundry day, a spare pair in the bag, and a few pairs in rotation so each pair rests between wears and keeps its shape longer. One style in one colour, bought in bulk, is the simplest drawer to keep: pairs never go missing. <a href="/posts/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a> covers washing and rotation.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are crew socks or ankle socks better for everyday?</summary><div class="a">Neither is better in general — it depends on the shoe and the day. Crew socks suit boots, trousers and cold weather; ankle and no-show socks suit trainers, shorts and the gym. The mistake is wearing one height for everything: a crew sock bunched into a low trainer, or an ankle sock under trousers that shows bare leg. The full comparison is in <a href="/posts/crew-socks-vs-ankle-socks/">Crew Socks vs Ankle Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are expensive socks worth it for everyday wear?</summary><div class="a">Sometimes — but the maths depends on how long they last. A premium merino crew costs as much as a six-pack of budget socks, yet Darn Tough replaces any pair free for life, and a well-made merino crew can outlast several cheap pairs. The honest answer is to split the drawer: a few premium pairs for the long days, and a cheap multi-pack for the ordinary ones. The cheapest way to stock a drawer is the Dickies or Gold Toe pick above.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What socks are best for standing all day?</summary><div class="a">A full-cushion crew with arch support — the Dickies pick above is the budget answer, and the merino Darn Tough crew is the premium one. Padding under the ball and heel takes the sting out of hard floors, and a sock that stays up means you stop thinking about your feet by midday. For jobs in safety boots, the work-specific picks are in <a href="/posts/best-work-socks/">Best Work Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How often should I replace everyday socks?</summary><div class="a">Everyday socks last roughly 6 to 12 months of regular wear — replace them when they thin at the heel or toe, lose their elastic, or keep their smell after washing, whichever comes first. Washing cold and air-drying stretches that out, because heat is what kills elastic. The full care and replacement guide is in <a href="/posts/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a>.</div></details>
