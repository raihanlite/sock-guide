## FAQ

**Should work-boot socks be thick or thin?** Neither by default — they should match the boot's internal volume and your floor. Thick, cushioned socks suit roomy boots and concrete; thinner socks suit snug boots and heat. The test: with the sock on, you should be able to wiggle your toes freely inside the laced boot. That kind of fit check is step five of [How to Choose the Right Socks](/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/).

**Do I need special socks for steel-toe boots?** Padding over the toes and a reinforced toe box genuinely help, because the cap creates a hard edge ordinary socks wear against. Any pick above with toe padding — Steely Boot, Carhartt Steel Toe, Fox River in our work-socks guide — covers it.

**Why do my socks slip down inside my boots?** Usually the sock is too short for the boot, or the elastic has died. Choose a boot-height sock with a proper heel pocket in your actual size — sizing up for "more room" is the most common cause of bunching and slip. Height matters as much as size here; see [Crew Socks vs Ankle Socks](/posts/crew-socks-vs-ankle-socks/) for how the lengths compare.

**Are women's work socks actually different?** Yes. They're knitted on a narrower last with a smaller heel pocket and less toe volume. In a women's boot, a scaled-down men's sock leaves loose fabric that folds and rubs; a women's-specific sock sits flat.
