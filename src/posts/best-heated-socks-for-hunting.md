## Short answer
A hunting sit outlasts a ski run, so **runtime on low is the number that matters**. Fieldsheer's Merino 2.0 gives 16+ hours on low for $99.99 — the longest runtime per dollar in the guides we read. For a premium pick, **Gerbing 7V Heated Wool Socks** are Field &amp; Stream's best overall; for budget, **Snow Deer** is the pick across several guides.

## How to choose heated socks for hunting
- **Runtime on low:** you need 10+ hours for a dark-to-dark sit. Ignore “max heat” runtime — you will run them on low most of the day.
- **Battery size vs bulk:** bigger batteries last longer but add bulk at the calf; slim batteries fit tight boots better.
- **Heat zones:** toe-focused is standard; full-sole coverage warms more evenly.
- **Material:** merino blends manage sweat better than cotton blends.
- **Controls:** phone/app control lets you change heat without taking boots off; a simple button is more reliable in the cold.

## FAQ
**How long do heated socks really last?** Ratings are usually measured indoors; cold drains batteries faster. Treat a “16-hour” rating as a long day, not a guarantee at 10°F.
**Can I wash heated socks?** Yes, with batteries removed, on a gentle cold cycle — check the brand's instructions first.
**Are heated socks safe?** Reputable brands use low-voltage (3.7–7.4V) batteries with multiple heat levels and auto shut-off. Never use damaged batteries.
