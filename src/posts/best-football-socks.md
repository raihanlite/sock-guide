Football socks do a different job from everyday socks, and the difference is not the badge on the calf. Inside a boot, your foot slides — a little on every cut, sprint and strike, and a lot more once sweat builds up. A grip sock puts rubber or silicone pads between your foot and the boot to hold it still, which also cuts down the rubbing that causes blisters. A match sock is long — over the calf — because it has to hold a shin pad in place and meet the kit rules. A training sock is the one you can afford to own five pairs of and wash every other day. So this guide picks by need: four grip socks for inside the boot, one match sock and one training sock. The honest line from FootballBoots.co.uk, who have tested hundreds of grip socks, is worth keeping in mind: the right pair will not suddenly make you a better player, but it can make a well-fitted boot feel even better. For the background first — heights, fits and what each type is for — start with [Types of Socks](/posts/types-of-socks/) and [How to Choose the Right Socks](/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/). The fibre-by-fibre case for each material is in [Sock Fabrics](/posts/sock-fabrics/), and the sweaty-feet picks are in [Best Socks for Sweaty Feet](/posts/best-socks-for-sweaty-feet/).

The short version: TRUsox for the strongest grip, Nike's new VaporFast for Nike boots, Tapedesign for comfort and wide boots, Gain The Edge for a first pair at a lower price, Nike Academy for matches, and the adidas Copa Zone for training. One fit rule decides most of it: thin grip socks suit snug speed boots, thicker ones suit roomy leather boots — a thick sock in a tight boot squeezes the foot and does more harm than good.

## FAQ
<details class="faq"><summary>What socks do professional footballers wear?</summary><div class="a">Most professionals wear a grip sock on the foot — TRUsox, Tapedesign and NikeGrip are the names that come up most often — under a cut-off team sock. The grip sock stops the foot sliding inside the boot; the team sock, cut at the ankle, covers the shin pad and shows the club colours. That is why you see the dotted TRUsox pattern on players' calves: it is the grip sock showing where the team sock was cut away.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Do grip socks really help?</summary><div class="a">They help with one specific problem: your foot sliding inside the boot when you cut, sprint or strike, which also reduces the rubbing that causes blisters. What they do not do is make you a better player — FootballBoots.co.uk puts it plainly after testing hundreds of pairs: the right pair will not suddenly improve your game, but it can make a well-fitted boot feel even better. If your boots fit well and your feet stay put already, a grip sock changes little; if your heel lifts or your toes slide on every cut, it changes a lot.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How do you wear grip socks with shin pads and team socks?</summary><div class="a">The professional setup, called the cut-sock technique: put the grip sock on first, then the shin pad, then cut your team sock (or a leg sleeve) at the ankle and pull it over the pad. The grip sock grips the boot, the cut team sock holds the pad and shows the kit colours, and tape around the top of the pad keeps everything from slipping. Nike's new VaporFast line is built around exactly this habit, with separate crew socks and leg sleeves so players no longer need to cut their socks. For training, most players skip the cutting and just wear the grip sock under a normal long sock.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What socks should I wear under shin pads?</summary><div class="a">A long sock — over the calf — that holds the pad against your leg, or a grip sock plus a shin-pad sleeve. The Nike Academy and adidas Copa Zone picks above are both over-the-calf for exactly this reason. A short ankle sock will not hold a pad: it slides down and the pad follows it. If the pad still moves, tape around the top of the sock (never directly on skin) is the usual fix.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are grip socks worth it for kids or Sunday league?</summary><div class="a">For a child, start cheap: the Gain The Edge pick comes in kids' sizes and costs roughly half what the premium grip socks do, and a young player outgrows socks before wearing them out anyway. For Sunday league, the same logic applies — a value grip sock plus a long match sock is the whole setup, and the expensive pairs only earn their price if you play often enough to feel the difference. What matters more than the brand is the fit rule: a thin grip sock in a snug boot, a thicker one in a roomy boot.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How do I wash grip socks so the grip lasts?</summary><div class="a">Turn them inside out before washing, wash cold on a gentle cycle, and air-dry them — heat is what kills the grip pads and the elastic. Do not use fabric softener, which coats the pads, and do not tumble-dry them. Washed this way, the pads on a good pair survive a full season of weekly games; washed hot and dried in a machine, they peel and harden much sooner. The general care rules are in <a href="/posts/how-to-take-care-of-socks/">How to Take Care of Socks</a>.</div></details>
