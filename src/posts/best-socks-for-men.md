There is no separate sock technology for men. The fibres and knits are the same — what differs is sizing, sold by men's shoe size, and use: most men need a small rotation for the office, the work boot, the run and the cold day. So this guide picks by use, not by marketing: six socks, one for each job a drawer has to do.

The short version: a cotton-blend half calf for everyday, a cheap cushioned crew pack for work, a merino crew for long days, a thin dress sock for suits, a running no-show for miles, and a thick merino crew for cold weather. If your feet sweat a lot, the rotation changes — see [Best Socks for Sweaty Feet](/posts/best-socks-for-sweaty-feet/), and the fibre case is in [Sock Fabrics](/posts/sock-fabrics/). The women's version is [Best Socks for Women](/posts/best-socks-for-women/); for background, start with [Types of Socks](/posts/types-of-socks/) and [How to Choose the Right Socks](/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/), or just the everyday picks in [Best Everyday Socks](/posts/best-everyday-socks/).

## FAQ
<details class="faq"><summary>What socks should men wear every day?</summary><div class="a">A midweight crew or half calf in a cotton blend or merino, matched to the shoe and the day: a cotton blend for the office and dry days, merino if your feet sweat or the day runs long. Thin, smooth dress socks belong with dress shoes; thick hiking socks belong in boots, not in slim trainers. The step-by-step choice is in <a href="/posts/how-to-choose-the-right-socks/">How to Choose the Right Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>How many pairs of socks should a man own?</summary><div class="a">At least one pair for each day you wear shoes, plus spares — a week's worth is a practical minimum, more if you exercise daily or work long shifts. Add a few dress pairs for suits and a few sport pairs for running or the gym, so the everyday pairs are not doing every job. Rotating pairs also makes each pair last longer: the knit recovers its shape between wears.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What socks go with dress shoes?</summary><div class="a">Thin dress socks in an over-the-calf or extended crew height, so no bare leg shows when you sit. Match the socks to your trousers, not your shoes — dark navy or charcoal with a navy suit, black with black trousers. Keep the fabric fine enough not to bulk out a slim shoe. The Mack Weldon pick above is built for this; the height rules are in <a href="/posts/types-of-socks/">Types of Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Are ankle socks or crew socks better for men?</summary><div class="a">Neither is better in general — it depends on the shoe and the day. Crew socks suit boots, trousers and cold weather; ankle and no-show socks suit trainers, shorts and the gym. The mistake is wearing one height for everything: a crew sock bunched into a low trainer, or an ankle sock under trousers that shows bare leg. The full comparison is in <a href="/posts/crew-socks-vs-ankle-socks/">Crew Socks vs Ankle Socks</a>.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>Do men need different socks for running?</summary><div class="a">Yes — running socks are built to manage sweat, hold their fit and prevent blisters, which everyday cotton crews do not do: cotton stays wet, bunches and rubs over miles. A running-specific sock (the Darn Tough pick above, or any of the six in <a href="/posts/best-running-socks/">Best Running Socks</a>) is the cheapest upgrade in a runner's kit.</div></details>

<details class="faq"><summary>What size socks do men wear?</summary><div class="a">Men's socks are sold by US men's shoe size, and every maker prints its own size chart — check it before ordering, because the ranges differ. Darn Tough runs S to XXL, Bombas runs M to XL, and Mack Weldon's Everyday line comes in one size only. If you sit between sizes, size up: Amazon's own summary flags the Darn Tough Standard Crew as running small.</div></details>
